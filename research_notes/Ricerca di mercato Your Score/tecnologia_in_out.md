# Electronic line calling (in/out) and court video technology for racket sports: vendor map, pricing, certification, gaps (as of October 2026)

Method note: research done 22 Oct 2026 using web search. Full-page fetches failed (DNS errors on itftennis.com, pickleball.com, sportico.com, sportsvideo.org), so the findings below come from search-result summaries of the cited pages, not from reading each page in full. Treat exact figures as needing a check against the original URL before they go into a pitch deck. "Vendor claim" marks figures that come from the company itself or from an interested party.

## 1. Pro level: Hawk-Eye, Foxtenn, Bolt6, IMG Arena, PlayReplay, Owl AI (who uses them, cost, setup, certification)

### Takeaway
At the top of tennis, four ITF "Gold" systems (Hawk-Eye/Sony, Foxtenn, Bolt6 Sentinel, IMG Arena) dominate. They cost tens of thousands of dollars or euros per court per event, and the ATP has used ELC at all Tour events since 2025. In pro pickleball, 2026 is the first year of electronic line calling: PlayReplay (an ITF Silver system) runs on the PPA/UPA side and Owl AI (software on top of broadcast cameras) at MLP. In padel, Premier Padel uses camera-based video review with a challenge allotment rather than a certified ELC system.

### Cited Findings
**Tennis, top level**
- The ITF classified list shows Hawk-Eye, Foxtenn, IMG Arena and Bolt6 (Sentinel) as Gold, and PlayReplay and Zenniz Smart Court as Silver. Each classification is tied to a specific setting, surface and mode (review or real-time). — [ITF Classified ELC Systems](https://www.itftennis.com/en/about-us/tennis-tech/classified-elc-systems/)
- The ATP said in 2023 that all ATP Tour events would switch to electronic line calling by 2025. — [Wikipedia, Electronic line judge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_line_judge); [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/sport/tennis/electronic-line-calls-at-all-atp-mens-tennis-events-by-2025-1.1682770046354)
- Wimbledon replaced line judges with ELC from 2025. — [Al Jazeera, Oct 2024](https://www.aljazeera.com/sports/2024/10/9/wimbledon-to-replace-tennis-line-judges-with-electronic-system-from-2025)
- Hawk-Eye ELC tracks the ball with about 18 cameras around the court. — [Yahoo Sports explainer](https://sports.yahoo.com/electronic-line-calling-elc-wimbledon-130551064.html)
- **Hawk-Eye cost, from press reports (not official price lists, and the figures conflict):**
  - $25,000 per court for Hawk-Eye Live on five courts (Tennis.com, 2021). — [Tennis.com](https://www.tennis.com/news/articles/gets-the-call-right-every-time-how-tennis-went-electronic)
  - $60,000–70,000 to fit one court with Hawk-Eye Live. — [ESPN, 2021](https://www.espn.com/tennis/story/_/id/30877297/hawk-eye-live-gains-more-support-australian-open)
  - Hawk-Eye told CNBC that one court's equipment costs nearly $100,000 and takes about three days to set up. — [CNBC, Sep 2023](https://www.cnbc.com/2023/09/09/how-sonys-hawk-eye-works-at-the-us-open.html)

  - Another source puts Hawk-Eye-type systems at an estimated €60–70k per court per week. That figure comes from an article promoting a rival vendor, eyes3. — [LA Wire](https://lawire.com/this-technology-has-arrived-to-challenge-hawk-eye/)
- **Foxtenn:** it uses about 40 cameras plus scanners and lasers and claims to capture the actual impact rather than simulate it. It has been the main ELC system used on clay and reportedly achieved a "zero error" rating in ATP/WTA/ITF testing. — [Perfect Tennis](https://www.perfect-tennis.com/why-there-is-no-hawk-eye-on-clay/); [Infobae/Around the Rings, Jul 2021](https://www.infobae.com/aroundtherings/articles/2021/07/12/foxtenn-electronic-line-calling-system-makes-the-grade)

**Padel, top level (Premier Padel / FIP)**
- Premier Padel uses video review to support the chair umpire. Each pair gets 2 challenges per set and loses one if the review goes against them. Reviews mostly cover in/out off the glass and net invasion. A video referee picks the camera angle and talks to the umpire by headset. The source article is from about 2022. — [Milano Premier Padel P1](https://www.milanopremierpadel.com/en/revision-de-video-video-review-between-high-technology-and-fair-play-among-players/)
- Padel press reports that Foxtenn has been used in pro padel, describes it as "high frequency radar"-based, notes a delay before results appear, and reports a debate about going back to traditional video replay. — [Padel Magazine UK](https://padel-magazine.co.uk/foxtenn-sometimes-decries-by-players-the-return-of-traditional-replay/)
- FIP/Premier Padel named Sportradar as integrity and official betting data partner in Sep 2026. This shows official data and video rights are becoming monetisable assets in padel. — [padelfip.com, Sep 2026](https://www.padelfip.com/2026/09/premier-padel-and-fip-appoint-sportradar-as-integrity-partner-as-sport-establishes-official-betting-data-ecosystem/)

**Pickleball, top level**
- Pickleball Inc. (the parent of PPA/UPA) partnered with PlayReplay as exclusive worldwide distributor of PlayReplay's technology for pickleball. The plan is pro events first, then clubs and public facilities. — [Pickleball.com](https://pickleball.com/news/pickleball-inc-partners-with-playreplay-to-bring-automated-line-calls-and-ai-tracking-to-the-sport-of-pickleball)
- PlayReplay offers a Review mode (players challenge and see the replay) and a Live mode (real-time calls). Rollout started at the PPA Finals in San Clemente (from 4 May), and 8 courts at the Veolia Nationals had ELC (PPA CEO Connor Pardoe). — [The Kitchen Pickle](https://thekitchenpickle.com/blogs/news/pickleball-automated-line-calls-ppa-tour-major-league-pickleball/)
- PPA 2026 rules extend electronic review to every streamed court at Slams and Worlds, with a fixed challenge allotment per match. — [PPA Tour 2026 rule changes](https://www.ppatour.com/2026-rule-changes/)

- **Hardware conflict:** the partnership announcement describes 4 cameras on the net posts, while a March 2026 hands-on describes 2 cameras, one at each end of the net. — [Pickleball.com](https://pickleball.com/news/pickleball-inc-partners-with-playreplay-to-bring-automated-line-calls-and-ai-tracking-to-the-sport-of-pickleball); [The Kitchen Pickle](https://thekitchenpickle.com/blogs/news/pickleball-automated-line-calls-ppa-tour-major-league-pickleball/)
- **MLP and Owl AI:** MLP announced a partnership with Owl AI in Dec 2025 for automatic line calling and an upgraded challenge system in 2026, working alongside the referees. Owl AI was founded in May 2025 and previously worked on X Games judging. Its software sits behind standard or broadcast cameras and runs in the cloud. — [Pickleball.com](https://pickleball.com/news/mlp-announces-partnership-with-owl-ai-to-streamline-line-calls-and-video-challenges); [MLP release](https://majorleaguepickleball.co/?p=117774); [The Kitchen Pickle](https://thekitchenpickle.com/blogs/news/major-league-pickleball-owl-ai-automatic-line-calls)

It was first tested at the PPA Greater Zion Cup in March. — [SVG / SportsVideo.org, 22 May 2026](https://www.sportsvideo.org/2026/05/22/owl-ai-and-major-league-pickleball-go-live-with-first-ever-ai-officiating-system-powered-by-broadcast-cameras-and-the-cloud/)

The MLP release is more authoritative. One possible explanation is that both were used on different courts or functions, but that is unverified. — [The Kitchen Pickle](https://thekitchenpickle.com/blogs/news/pickleball-automated-line-calls-ppa-tour-major-league-pickleball/) vs [MLP release](https://majorleaguepickleball.co/?p=117774)

### Inferences
- Gold-tier tennis ELC costs roughly $25k–100k per court and needs on-site technical staff and multi-day setup. That is out of reach for mid-level events, which is the price gap the ITF's lower tiers are meant to address.
- Pickleball's pro tours took two different routes: dedicated court hardware (PlayReplay) and software on existing broadcast cameras (Owl AI). The Owl AI model shows that ~4 cameras plus software is now accepted at pro level. That supports the Your Score architecture, but competitors can copy it quickly.
- Padel has no widely adopted, certified automatic line-calling system at pro level. Glass rebounds make padel rules different from tennis "in/out" (the key calls are the first bounce in the box and the ball hitting the glass or fence). This is both a technical barrier and an opening.

## 2. Club and consumer level: PlaySight, Wingfield, Zenniz, SwingVision, In/Out, Baseline Vision, padel camera systems (prices and functions)

### Takeaway
The club market splits in two. (a) Tennis "smart net post" or line-calling products (PlayReplay at about $3.5–4.5k per court per year, Zenniz, Wingfield, plus low-cost devices like Baseline Vision at ~$2,000 and In/Out at ~$350–550) do line calling and stats. (b) Padel camera, replay and highlight systems (GameCam, PUSHIT, PlaySight, CourtMaster, Clutch, Save My Play, MATCHi TV) cost about €50–250 per court per month and focus on recording, highlights and streaming, not certified in/out calls.

### Cited Findings
**Tennis / pickleball line calling for clubs**
- PlayReplay raised $12M (2026) for line calling on local tennis courts. It charges facilities **$3,500–4,500 per court per year**, pitches that clubs can raise booking and tournament fees, and planned to expand to pickleball. — [Sportico, 2026](https://www.sportico.com/business/tech/2026/tennis-electronic-line-calling-startup-playreplay-value-1234891784/)
- ITF gave PlayReplay real-time Silver-level status on hard courts in Feb 2026. — [ITF news](https://www.itftennis.com/en/news-and-media/articles/playreplay-electronic-line-calling-system-achieves-real-time-silver-status)
- College tennis (ITA) only approves ELC systems that hold the appropriate ITF classification. The ITA list showed PlayReplay. — [ITA ELC page](https://wearecollegetennis.com/electronic-line-calling/)
- Zenniz is an ITF-classified (Silver, "Smart Court") net-post system that uses "Dual Tracking" (audio plus camera) for line calls and analytics. Its player app features were free in 2024. Its reported funding is $8.2M in one source and $12.0M (May 2026) in another. — [TennisLeo 2026](https://www.tennisleo.com/best-tennis-line-calling-and-smart-net-post-systems/); [TennisLeo Zenniz review](https://www.tennisleo.com/zenniz-review/); [VCBacked](https://www.vcbacked.co/directory/industries/tennis)
- Wingfield is an AI-powered smart net post that records matches and provides analytics. No price was found. — [TennisLeo 2026](https://www.tennisleo.com/best-tennis-line-calling-and-smart-net-post-systems/)
- SwingVision is a phone/iPad app with live line calling and stats. Pro costs $179.99 per year or $14.99 per month (US App Store). It raised a $6M Series A to bring line calling to junior tournaments, adult leagues and college tennis. It has also been tested in tournament play. — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/US/app/id989461317); [SwingVision Series A](https://swing.vision/newsletters/series-a-financing); [Tennis.com](https://www.tennis.com/news/articles/fair-play-swingvision-puts-its-electronic-line-calling-to-the-test-in-tournament-play)

Pickleball support has been added, and it was reviewed again in Apr 2026. — [Gadgets & Wearables, Apr 2026](https://gadgetsandwearables.com/2026/04/26/in-out-tennis-line-calling/); [CNN, 2017 ($199 original)](https://www.cnn.com/2017/03/21/tennis/tennis-tech-gadget)

### Inferences
- Price anchors in Europe: padel replay and highlights cost **€50–250 per court per month**. Club-grade certified line calling (PlayReplay) costs about **€270–380 per court per month** (from $3.5–4.5k per year). A Your Score system with 4 cameras plus a tablet would need to land around or below the PlayReplay price, with replay included, to compete.
- None of the padel camera vendors found offer an ITF-style certified automatic in/out call. They offer replay, VAR-style review and highlights. The line-calling piece is what would set Your Score apart in padel, and replay alone is already a crowded commodity.
- The result-entry tablet overlaps with booking and score platforms (Playtomic, MATCHi) and with the scoreboards on camera systems. Integration with Playtomic, as PlaySight does, matters for distribution.

### Gaps
- No published prices found for Zenniz, Wingfield, PlaySight, Pixellot, Reev, Mojo, ReplayPadel or Padelbox. Reev, Mojo, ReplayPadel and Padelbox did not appear in any search results.
- No independent accuracy benchmarks for club-level systems.

## 4. Federation certification and approval (ITF, FIP, USA Pickleball / IPF)

### Takeaway
Only tennis has a formal certification process: the ITF ELC classification, with Gold, Silver and Bronze tiers since July 2025. It is evaluated per surface, setting and mode, and it is "rigorous". Padel (FIP) and pickleball (USA Pickleball / IPF) have no public ELC approval scheme. In those sports, adoption is decided by the tour or league operator (Premier Padel, PPA, MLP) through commercial partnerships.

### Cited Findings
- Systems get ITF classification after a "rigorous evaluation process". Gold, Silver or Bronze depends on how well the system meets the criteria, and the classification applies per setting, surface and review/real-time mode. — [ITF Classified ELC](https://www.itftennis.com/en/about-us/tennis-tech/classified-elc-systems/)
- The tiers were introduced in July 2025. Gold is for elite competition (Grand Slams, ATP/WTA), Silver for the next level, and Bronze for national-level competitions. The aim is to widen access to ELC at more levels of tennis. — [ITF news](https://www.itftennis.com/en/news-and-media/articles/new-classification-tiers-for-electronic-line-calling-systems/); [ESPN UK](https://www.espn.co.uk/tennis/story/_/id/45802019/itf-introduces-tiered-system-expand-electronic-line-calling); [Tennis Tour Talk](https://tennistourtalk.com/124507/new-classification-tiers-for-electronic-line-calling-systems-create-access-to-technology-at-more-levels-of-tennis)
- The ITF publishes its evaluation procedures, currently v2.81 (Jan 2026), and an older evaluation paper. — [ITF ELC Evaluation Procedures v2.81, Jan 2026 (PDF)](https://www.itftennis.com/media/15990/elc-evaluation-procedures-v281-jan-2026.pdf); [ELC evaluation paper rev. 26 (PDF)](https://m.itftennis.com/media/7365/elc-evaluation-paper-revision-26.pdf)
- USTA points to ITF Silver vendors for ELC use (contact elc@usta.com), and ITA requires ITF classification. — [USTA ELC](https://www.usta.com/en/home/coach-organize/tennis-tool-center/electronic-line-calling.html); [ITA](https://wearecollegetennis.com/electronic-line-calling/)
- USA Pickleball's 2026 rule changes (effective 1 Jan 2026) cover manual line-call conduct ("out" must be called immediately). No electronic line-calling approval provision was found. — [PlayPickleball.com](https://playpickleball.com/2026-usa-pickleball-rule-changes-whats-changing-this-year/)
- The Premier Padel rulebook (Mar 2025) exists, but no ELC approval scheme was found in the search results. — [Premier Padel Rulebook 2025 (PDF)](https://www.padelfip.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/Premier-Padel-Rulebook-Men%C2%B4s_15_03_25_EN-1.pdf)

### Inferences
- For tennis, Bronze is the realistic target tier for Your Score, since it is meant for national-level competitions. It requires formal ITF testing, and fees and timelines are not public. The two Silver systems (PlayReplay, Zenniz) are funded companies with $8–12M+ raised, which suggests certification needs significant R&D investment.
- For padel and pickleball in Europe, there is no certification barrier. The gate is commercial (FIP or national federations, tournament organisers, and players' trust). Going to market with a "review/replay assistant" rather than an "official call" lowers the bar.

### Gaps
- ITF evaluation fees, test duration, and whether the procedures PDF sets precise error tolerances (not read in full because the fetch failed).
- Whether any Bronze-classified systems exist as of Oct 2026: not shown in the search snippets.
- FIP or national padel federation (FITP in Italy) rules on video review at lower-level tournaments: not found.

## 5. Revenue models

### Takeaway
Revenue models seen in the market: court hardware sold or leased plus an annual or monthly per-court subscription (PlayReplay, GameCam, PUSHIT, Save My Play, PlaySight). Per-clip or per-user fees paid by players (PlaySight highlights under €5, SwingVision Pro $180 per year). Club revenue uplift through higher booking or tournament fees (PlayReplay pitch). Live streaming with sponsor overlays (PUSHIT). Official data and betting rights at pro level (Sportradar and FIP).

### Cited Findings
- PlayReplay charges $3.5–4.5k per court per year, and clubs recover the cost by raising reservation and tournament prices (vendor pitch). — [Sportico](https://www.sportico.com/business/tech/2026/tennis-electronic-line-calling-startup-playreplay-value-1234891784/)
- GameCam uses hardware-as-a-service at €3k per court per year. PUSHIT has subscription tiers at €59–189 per court per month with sponsor overlays. Save My Play combines a hardware fee with a monthly SaaS fee. — [Authic](https://www.authic.io/blog/the-9-best-ai-camera-systems-for-padel-clubs-in-2026)
- PlaySight highlights are paid per clip by players (under €5). — [EIN Presswire](https://www.einpresswire.com/article/761571456/playsight-launches-automated-ai-highlights-to-enhance-player-experience-and-enable-easy-review-and-sharing)
- SwingVision uses a consumer subscription. — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/US/app/id989461317)
- At pro level, Hawk-Eye charges per court per event (see section 1), and data and betting rights are monetised (Sportradar and FIP, Sep 2026). — [padelfip.com](https://www.padelfip.com/2026/09/premier-padel-and-fip-appoint-sportradar-as-integrity-partner-as-sport-establishes-official-betting-data-ecosystem/)

### Inferences
- A good fit for Your Score: (1) a per-court monthly subscription for clubs (tablet plus replay), (2) per-event rental of a portable 4-camera kit for mid-level tournaments, (3) player-paid highlight clips, and (4) sponsor overlays on the tablet and stream. Combining these lets the price undercut PlayReplay while staying above pure-replay vendors.

### Gaps
- No public data on per-event rental pricing for portable ELC at amateur or mid-level tournaments in Europe.

## 6. Where is the gap: affordable line calling plus challenge replay for mid-level tournaments in Europe

### Takeaway
There is a visible gap between Gold-tier pro systems (tens of thousands per court per event) and club camera systems that only do replay and highlights. In tennis, PlayReplay and Zenniz (ITF Silver) already target that middle and have funding. In padel, no certified or widely adopted automatic in/out system exists below Premier Padel, and lower-tier events have little or no video review. In European pickleball, no local supplier was found. A portable, affordable "challenge replay plus assisted line call" kit with score tablets for padel, pickleball and amateur tennis tournaments looks open, provided accuracy is proven and it fits with booking platforms.

### Cited Findings
- The ITF created its lower tiers explicitly to bring ELC to more levels of tennis, which confirms that unmet demand below elite level is recognised. — [ITF](https://www.itftennis.com/en/news-and-media/articles/new-classification-tiers-for-electronic-line-calling-systems/)

### Inferences
- **Best entry point:** padel tournaments (FIP/Cupra lower tiers, national federation and club circuits in Italy and Spain), using a portable 4-camera kit rented per event, with challenge replay shown on the courtside tablet and the score synced. No certification is needed, and padel volumes in Italy and Spain are large.
- **Risks:** (1) Padel line calling with glass and fence rebounds is technically harder than tennis or pickleball. (2) Camera vendors (PlaySight, GameCam, PUSHIT) could add AI in/out calls. (3) PlayReplay or Owl AI could enter Europe and padel. (4) Without independent accuracy validation, players will not trust automated calls, so start with "replay for the umpire or players to decide" and add automated calls later.
- For tennis, mid-level events increasingly expect ITF-classified systems (ITA already requires them), so Bronze certification would eventually be needed.
